Why education is part of health

Health isn't only about what happens in a doctor's office.

Having information you can understand matters. Knowing where to find help matters. Having the skills to care for yourself or support someone else matters too.

Education can help make those things easier to access.

It's one more way we work to break down barriers to health and create more opportunities for people to live their healthiest lives.

Questions

What people ask before they start.

Getting started
Where do I actually start?

It depends what you came for. Courses and training live in the HMC Hub. UNBOXED and our articles are on the main site and need no account. Research reports on this page open straight to the file.

Do I need an account?

For a course, yes, because your progress and completion record have to attach to someone. To read an article, listen to UNBOXED or open a report, no.

Who can take part?

Community education is open to anyone. Some courses are written with a particular group in mind, such as youth exploring health careers, and professional training assumes you are working in or entering the field. Each one says who it is for.

What does it cost?

It varies by program. A good deal of our community education is offered at no cost, and some programming, particularly professional training and training delivered to an organization, is paid. The cost is stated with each course or training rather than here, so what you read is the current price and not last year's.

If cost is what is stopping you, tell us. It is worth asking.

Courses, training and credentials
What do I get at the end?

A Health Matters Clinic completion record naming what you completed, the assessment you passed and the date. Where you served in the field, the hours are on it.

Does this certify me as a Community Health Worker?

No, and be careful of anyone who says theirs does. California has no state Community Health Worker certification, and the Department of Health Care Services does not approve certificates. What an HMC completion record does is document real coursework and real hours, which is what employers and programs generally ask to see.

What is the difference between a course and a training?

A course is something you work through yourself, at your own pace. A training is delivered by people, usually to a group, and is built around the skills that group needs. Plenty of people do both.

Do you offer continuing education credit?

Where a session carries board approval, the boards it is approved for are named on that session. Where it does not, it says so. We do not describe a session as continuing education unless it is.

Bringing this to your organization
Can you train our staff, members or volunteers?

Yes, and this is a large part of what our training does. Tell us what you are working on, who you are trying to reach and roughly how many people, and we will tell you what fits.

Start at healthmatters.clinic/contact-us.

Can we use your curriculum ourselves?

In some cases, yes. The Unstoppable curriculum has been delivered to community groups and presented to the Los Angeles County Department of Mental Health, and facilitator preparation exists so your own people can deliver it. Ask us and we will be straight with you about what transfers and what needs us in the room.

Can we sponsor education for a community that could not otherwise pay?

Yes, and it is the most direct way to make a program free for the people in it. Get in touch and we will show you what a sponsored cohort covers.

UNBOXED, articles and research
Where can I listen to UNBOXED?

Every episode is at healthmatters.clinic/unboxed, to watch or to listen to.

Can I be a guest?

We would like to hear from you. Tell us what you would want to talk about and why it matters to you.

Can I cite or share your reports?

Yes. They are published to be read and used, and every one on this page opens as a file you can keep. If you are citing one in your own work and want to check a detail, ask us.
